Ten new widgets: mock, round 4 (approved at 8/10)

Every card is drawn at its true size on a 1920 × 1080 wall (12 × 8 cells, 147 × 121 px plus 12 px gaps). Scene: Phoenix, Friday 2 October 2026, 3:50 PM. Every chart and the words above it come from one data series in the script.

Times: "4:55 PM", ":00" dropped ("1 PM"), never split across lines. Days: Today, Tomorrow, the weekday within six days ("Sunday"), then "Wed 14 Oct". Spans: "25 min", "1 h 5 min", "3 days".
Colour: hero numbers are text colour. Accent marks only today and now. Six shared tag colours (calendars, reminders) and one severity scale (UV bands, quake magnitudes), all with light-theme values. Past data is drawn grey, not dimmed.
Overflow: problems and family-affecting things stay visible; holidays and healthy services collapse first. Cards: none of the ten has a title strip; each card names itself in its first line, states included. Motion: none, except a quake ping that runs four times when a new quake arrives (off under reduced motion). Rotations are minutes, not seconds.

1. Up next

Now and next from one or more calendars (a colour per person). The countdown to the next event is the hero. The rest of today is listed with times, ending in "Free after …". When nothing's left today it names tomorrow's first thing.

2. Week ahead

Seven columns, today first. Each day carries its weather; rain chance replaces the low when it's 30% or more. All-day events are one-line chips in up to two lanes, spanning their days; each column's timed events start right under its own chips. When lanes overflow, holidays (outlined) give way first and become "+1 all-day". Timed events show time and name (two lines) in their calendar colour.

8×3

5. Wind

A vane: the wedge sits inside the ring on the side the wind comes from, labelled with that side, and points in, matching "From the southwest". Speed in the middle, the Beaufort word as the headline, gusts as a second number that turns amber only when they're 10 mph over the wind. The next hours as compass letters.

4×2
3×1

6. Earthquakes

USGS, past 7 days, M2.5+ within 500 mi by default. The headline sums up; the list gives the three largest. Distance is linear (rings at 250 / 500 mi); quakes older than a day are rings, not dots; a quake very close to home is nudged outward along its true bearing so it doesn't sit on the house; dot area grows with magnitude, so an M4.3 looks much bigger than an M2.7. A new quake pings four times, then stays still.

7. Tides

NOAA predictions for the nearest station to home (or a chosen one). Which way the water's moving and when it turns, today's curve with each high and low, the past in grey. Predictions don't go out of date: once fetched, the days they cover keep drawing normally.

6×2
